Our Story

A family grill on a busy High Road

Pitta Corner sits at 43 High Road, a few minutes from Wood Green tube — a family-run Greek kitchen wedged into a parade of shops, with a dark blue front, a turquoise sign and window boxes spilling over with petunias and ivy. People spot the flowers first and the charcoal smell second.

Inside it works three ways at once. It's a coffee stop in the morning, a souvlaki counter at lunch, and a proper sit-down grill at night — coffee, crepes, breakfast, lunch and dinner, exactly as it says on the sign. The grill runs the whole time, in view of the counter, so the wrap in your hand was on the coals about ninety seconds ago.

The cooking is Greek and Cypriot home cooking scaled up for a takeaway queue: skewers seasoned with lemon and oregano, gyros carved off the spit, fries cut from whole potatoes, tzatziki and htipiti mixed in-house. Nothing clever. Just the things that are worth doing properly.

On The Grill

Four things we won't cut corners on

Read our menu board and the same words keep coming back — charcoal, hand cut, homemade. That's not copywriting, it's the prep list.

01

Charcoal kalamakia

Pork, chicken, beef, lamb and village loukaniko, threaded on skewers and cooked over coals. Served the traditional way — lemon, oregano, bread and cheese — from £2.50 a stick.

02

Hand-cut fries

Cut from whole potatoes every day, and they go inside the wrap, not next to it. That's the Greek way, and it's why the pittas come out heavier than you expect.

03

Homemade dips

Tzatziki, houmous, spicy feta htipiti and Russian salad, all mixed in our own kitchen. £2.99 a pot, and they're what most regulars add to the order without thinking.

04

Boards to share

The Mix Grill Platter is the table centrepiece — pittas, fries, dips and a run of skewers. £19.99 for two, £41.99 for four, and nobody has ever finished it hungry.
